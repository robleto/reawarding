# REAWARDING PRODUCT LAWS

## Law 1 — Awards Are The Face, Rankings Are The Engine

Users experience awards. Rankings are the power tool underneath — they fuel nominee formation, winner inference, and taste modeling. Rankings must never feel like required data entry or the primary emotional surface.

## Law 2 — Nominees Create Legitimacy

Awards require comparison. A single movie is a preference; a field of movies is a ballot. This stays as-is — it's foundational.

## Law 3 — Ballots Must Form, Never Appear

Ballots must always feel like they're forming from user taste. Onboarding seeds a nominee and demonstrates formation. The system may accelerate formation but must never skip it. Users must feel "I built this" not "the system generated this."

## Law 4 — Authority Emerges From Maturity

Ballot maturity (nominee count, expression depth) governs everything: language tone, visual weight, automation confidence, suggestion strength. Thin ballots are provisional. Mature ballots are canonical. Authority is earned, never assigned.

## Law 5 — The System Suggests, The User Owns

The system may infer, predict, and suggest — but never override explicit user choice, remove human authorship, or hide how conclusions were reached. Nothing is final until the user confirms.

## Law 6 — Awards Are Living Artifacts

Ballots evolve as ratings change, movies are added, and overrides occur. Awards are never generated once and frozen.

## Law 7 — One Year, One Canonical State

Each (year + category) resolves to one authoritative winner and one authoritative nominee field. The system may track provisional states internally, but the user always sees one canonical outcome.

## Law 8 — Identity Over Completion

Users are expressing taste, perspective, and retrospective judgment — not filling in history. Personal awards are canon. The system never validates against the Academy. Progress is measured in meaning, not completeness. Structure should feel institutional; growth should feel personal.
