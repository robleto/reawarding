# .impeccable.md — Reawarding

This file establishes the design context that governs all AI-assisted design work in this project. Read alongside `PRODUCT_LAWS.md`, `PRODUCT_DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md`, and `PROJECT_CONTEXT.md` — those are authoritative for product behavior; this file defines aesthetic intent.

---

## Design Context

### Users

Film-loving adults who want to *rewrite Oscar history* based on what they actually watched and how films aged in their memory. They are opinion-first, hindsight-aware, and emotionally invested in their taste — not completionists, not loggers, not stat-collectors.

**Primary use context:** Mobile, often after watching something. Quick taste-expression in stolen moments — couch, bed, commute. They are not sitting at a desk maintaining a database; they are reacting to a film they just saw or remembering one they love.

**Job to be done:** "Tell me my own Best Picture for 1999, and let me feel the weight of that choice." Express taste → see structure form → recognize their own canon.

### Brand Personality

**Three words: Curious. Editorial. Personal.**

- **Curious** — invites re-evaluation, asks "what if?", treats taste as exploration not competition
- **Editorial** — magazine-grade typography and rhythm, not dashboard density; chosen, not generated
- **Personal** — the user's canon, not the Academy's; awards belong to them

**Premium feel** is a goal — Apple Music / Apple TV-tier polish: large posters, cinematic dark surfaces, generous space, restrained motion. Reawarding has its own identity, but the *quality bar* is set by Apple's editorial properties.

**Emotional goal:** Validation and recognition. The user should feel the system *honors* their taste — never grades it, never scolds it for being incomplete, never gamifies it.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Theme:** Dark only. The gold + charcoal palette is canonical (gold `#D4AF37`, charcoal `#2D2D2D`). Light mode is not a goal.

**Typography:**
- **Unbounded** (display / headings / award names / years) — geometric, ceremonial, the "ReAward" voice
- **Inter** (body, UI, microcopy) — neutral and readable

**Visual mood:** Cinematic museum after-hours. Posters as primary visual currency. Gold reserved for *recognition moments* — winners, milestones, taste signals — never decorative chrome. Charcoal is the gallery wall.

**Anti-references — what Reawarding is explicitly NOT:**
- ❌ Letterboxd (logging-first, social-graph-first, dense feed of activity)
- ❌ IMDb (database aesthetic, completionist, ad-laden)
- ❌ Gamification UIs with badges, streaks, XP bars, level-up modals
- ❌ Generic SaaS dashboards (hero metric + grid of identical cards)
- ❌ The "AI slop" palette (cyan-on-dark, purple→blue gradient text, glass panels with glow borders)
- ❌ Stat walls and "you've watched 247 films this year" surfaces

### Design Principles

These are derived from `PRODUCT_DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md` and the conversation that produced this file. They govern every design decision.

1. **Posters are the visual currency** — In a film product, the poster is the most powerful UI element ever offered. Lean on them. Reduce competing chrome around them. A grid of posters with restrained typography beats any custom card system.

2. **Gold is for recognition, not decoration** — Gold appears on winners, milestones, and taste-identity moments. Never on borders, dividers, focus rings, or "premium-feeling" gradients. If gold is everywhere, gold means nothing.

3. **Formation is visible, ceremony is reserved** — Home/workshop shows ballots forming with rough edges. Awards page shows the finished artifact with weight. Never blur these — never show a fully-formed ballot on the workbench, never expose formation seams in the museum.

4. **Editorial restraint over dashboard density** — When in doubt, fewer elements with more space. The editorial press release feel (Apple Music album page, Criterion essay) beats the analytics-tile feel every time.

5. **Mobile is canonical, not adapted** — Most expression happens on phones. Touch targets ≥ 44px, thumb-zone primary actions, no hover-dependent affordances, no desktop layouts shrunk to fit. WCAG AA contrast minimum; reduced-motion respected.

6. **Home scales with maturity, Awards is always the archive** — Home and the Awards page differ in *intent* (Home is identity + active work; Awards is the full archive), but the dominant register of Home shifts as the user earns it. New and building users see a workbench. Established users see a workbench with rewards below. Mature users — established users who have *also* reached 3+ canonical ballots, 5+ years, or 50+ rated — see a museum with a fold-out workshop: a prominent "Welcome back, {name}." headline + full-width hero search, with a quiet "Update awards" text link that toggles an inline workshop drawer (year timeline + active ballot card). Below: Awards Gallery, Lists, Ready-Made, Watchlist, Recognition Feed, Canon. The workshop is always one tap away — no modal — but it stops dominating the surface for users who have earned the museum. Mature is strictly additive to established; never a leapfrog.

7. **Trust the user's taste before the system's confidence** — Authority emerges from maturity (Law 4). Thin ballots are visibly provisional, never dressed up as canonical. The system suggests; the user owns (Law 5).

## Quick Reference for Critique & Implementation

When reviewing or building:
- ✅ Does the gold appear earned, or sprinkled?
- ✅ Are posters carrying the visual weight, or are cards/chrome competing?
- ✅ Does this surface match the user's maturity? (workbench for new/building/established, museum-led with fold-out workshop for mature)
- ✅ On a 375px viewport, is the primary action in the thumb zone with a ≥44px target?
- ✅ Does typography use Unbounded for ceremony and Inter for clarity, or is it muddled?
- ✅ Would a user say "this honors my taste" — or "this is grading me"?
