# PRODUCT DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Milestones reshape the canvas — Critical moments persist on the page, never hidden in modals or toasts. 

Empty states feel intentional — Incomplete ballots feel forming or emerging, never broken. 

Emergent intelligence is visible — When taste becomes structured output, the system reveals it clearly. 

Leveling up, not unlocking — Major milestones feel like recognition and expansion, not checklist completion. 

Home's goal is the museum, not a preview of it — Home and the Awards page still differ in *intent* (Home is identity + active work; Awards is the full archive), but they share one surface: once a user has started any ballot, the awards showcase — the year archive, Best Picture, nominees, winner — is the primary content of Home, arrived at directly, not something unlocked at a maturity tier. New and building users with no set ballot still see a workbench (search + year timeline + one active ballot card) because there's nothing to showcase yet. Everyone else scrolls straight into their showcase; there is no separate "museum register" a user graduates into with a different Home layout. The New/Building/Established/Mature thresholds still govern which *rewards* sections are earned (Awards Gallery, Recognition Feed, Canon — see "Galleries earn their place" and "State thresholds measure depth, not breadth"), just not which Home layout renders.

Nudges earn their place, one at a time — The strip above the showcase carries at most one contextual line, chosen by relevance to this specific user right now, not a fixed per-tier checklist stacked underneath the welcome headline. Candidates, in priority order: a first-award moment (one-time, see below); a "finish what you started" prompt, shown only for years with an actionable next film — an unrated-but-seen title or a watchlisted one — because a veteran with nothing left to rate for an old year isn't unfinished, they're just caught up, and nagging them about it is noise; a "Ready-made lists" teaser, withheld until the user has earned Established, so three early ratings that happen to share a director don't get read as a pattern before it is one. The highest-priority eligible candidate renders; the rest wait their turn silently. Most sessions show zero or one line, never a stack of banners and chip rows competing for the same six inches of screen.

Creation starts from home — Users can always begin expressing taste from the primary surface.

Workshop shows formation, not ceremony — Structure building and direct manipulation in workshop; ceremony visuals reserved for award view.

Ballot status comes from nominee count — Emerging/Standard/Complete are computed states, not manual labels.

State thresholds measure depth, not breadth — A "year touched" (one rating in a year) is not the same as a "year invested in." The Established state requires at least two *depth years* (a year with 3+ ratings each), or one set ballot (5+ nominees), or 20+ total ratings. Counting years touched promotes shallow tourists into a museum register they haven't earned and produces hollow Awards Galleries. Breadth is cheap. Depth is what we measure.

Galleries earn their place — The Awards Gallery does not surface on Home until at least one year has a ballot with 3+ nominees. Below that bar, a year's "highest-rated film" is not a winner; it is a placeholder. Promoting placeholders to Best Picture cards is false copy. When no year clears the bar, the gallery is absent — not gated behind a lock, just absent. The page asks for something to celebrate before it celebrates.

Building has two leads — A user with a 2/5 ballot and a user with a 4/5 ballot are in different emotional states. The 2/5 user is forming; the 4/5 user is one rating from a set award and that is the highest-urgency moment in the app. The active ballot card varies its lead accordingly: forming ballots read "Your {year} ballot has N nominees. N more sets it." Near-set ballots (4/5) read "One more film completes your {year} ballot." The empty target slot is visually marked, not styled identically to the others.

Crossing into Established is a marked moment — The first time a user sets a ballot (5+ nominees + an explicit winner), the Established home renders a single persistent on-canvas line at the top: "{Year} is set. Your first award." Plain, specific, dismissible. Not a modal. Not a toast. Not confetti. The line stays until the user dismisses it; the Awards Gallery appears below it with exactly one entry. Subsequent ballot completions do not trigger this moment — it is reserved for the transition itself.

The screenshot-user test — A user with multiple shallow years and zero set ballots should not see an Awards Gallery, a Canon section, or a taste-chip rail on Home. They should see a year timeline, one active ballot card (the closest to set), and a search bar. Three things. If a section cannot answer the question "did the user earn this," it does not render at this engagement level.
